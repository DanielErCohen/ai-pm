Setup and support
Why it worksFew people read back the transcripts that record how they decide and follow up.
  • It counts decisions made versus deferred and follow-ups closed.
  • Every finding quotes your own words, with a date.
  • It ends with three habits to test next month, each with a count to check.
  • It never scores anyone else.

What you get

One review of about 3,000 words, built from your transcripts and read in date order.

  • What you do well and what to change, each backed by 2 or 3 dated quotes in your own words.
  • PM craft and team-lead craft: problem framing, use of evidence, decisions made versus deferred and follow-ups closed, then feedback, delegation, unblocking, airtime and how you run the meeting.
  • Your first month against your last, with counts.
  • Three things to try next month, each with a count to check it against.
  • The skill marks anything it infers as inferred.
  • Built as a general method: test it on your own transcripts. Work done outside meetings needs a separate check.

Setup steps

  1. Export your transcripts. Most meeting tools export .vtt, .txt or .docx, and a .md file works too. The speaker names must be in the file, because the skill has to tell your words from the others'. Three months gives a good read. Fewer than 6 meetings is too thin for patterns over time.
  2. Put them in one local folder, for example ~/meeting-transcripts/. A date in each file name, such as 2025-04-17-team-sync.vtt, saves the skill from guessing.
  3. Copy the skill with the command in the Claude Code or Claude app tab above.
  4. Run it. It asks which speaker labels are you and whether you want the PM view, the team-lead view or both. With more than 10 meetings it starts fast-model subagents to read the batches, and the main model writes the review. Before it writes, it checks every quote against its source file with an exact-text search, drops any that do not match and reports how many it dropped. It saves the review next to the folder as meeting-review-<date>.md.
  5. Clean up. Delete the transcript folder, which also holds _review-notes/. The review stays.

Privacy

Transcripts hold other people's words and personal data. Read these before you export anything.

  • Company policy and consent. Check your company's policy on recording, on storing transcripts and on using AI tools. Check the consent rules for the people in your meetings. Rules differ by country and by company.
  • Keep the export local. Use a folder that no shared drive or sync service picks up. The skill reads only the folder you name and searches nothing. The text still reaches the model provider you use with Claude, so the policy check covers that too.
  • Delete the export. Remove the transcript folder and _review-notes/ when you have the review.
  • The review describes you only. It quotes only your words, names other people by role, and skips candidate interviews and pay or performance conversations unless you say otherwise.
  • Read it before you share it. The review quotes your meetings and may carry company details.
